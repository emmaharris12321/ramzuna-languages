FAQs for Teachers

Everything volunteer teachers and conversation partners need to know about applying, teaching, and volunteering with us.

Home / FAQs for Teachers
Looking for answers as a student? Visit our FAQs for Students.

52 questions across 8 themes

01

Students & Learning

Who the students are, their goals, and the learning support provided.

Our students are all Palestinians. While most are in Gaza, we also accept students from the West Bank and those who have been displaced due to the war. Our students are predominantly aged between 13 and 30.

Students’ objectives vary according to their circumstances and needs. These may include improving language skills, preparing for official examinations, or strengthening their remote career prospects. We match students with teachers according to experience, qualifications, and teaching preferences.

Yes. We provide a textbook and a curated collection of teaching resources, although you are also welcome to use suitable resources of your own. At the start of the term, teachers receive learning objectives for their students.

Teachers are expected to assess their students. We provide the student’s initial level and a specific assessment resource for teachers to use. Conversation partners are not expected to conduct assessments; their partners’ levels are provided in advance.

Teachers receive a specific progress-recording resource, which they must complete by the end of the term.

02

Application & Safeguarding

The recruitment process, required training, and safeguarding checks.

After we review your application, you will be invited to complete training in trauma-informed practice and education in emergencies.

Once we have reviewed your responses and confirmed your understanding of the training, you will sign a volunteer agreement.

Finally, you will be invited to a group introductory meeting before you begin teaching. If your student is a minor, you will also need to provide a valid, current background check.

The training is a crucial part of the recruitment process. It explains potential triggers for students, offers strategies to minimise them, and provides guidance on teaching students from conflict-affected regions.

Yes. We welcome volunteers whose first language is not English, provided that they have an advanced level of English.

Volunteers need an advanced level of English so that they can communicate clearly and support students effectively.

No. All teaching and conversation-partner positions are entirely voluntary and unpaid.

Contact us as soon as possible, before the deadline. Because we receive a high volume of applications, failure to complete the training on time will usually mean that your application cannot progress.

Unfortunately, we cannot accept applications once the training deadline has passed. You are welcome to apply again for the next term when applications reopen.

No. Applications open twice a year, one month before the beginning of each term. We announce reopening dates on our website and social media.

If applications are closed, you can join the waiting list to express your interest. Teachers on the waiting list receive priority notification when applications reopen and may be contacted if a place becomes available earlier.

03

Lessons & Logistics

Lesson formats, scheduling, and the platforms used to teach.

Most students learn in a small group of three or four, which is our default format. One-to-one lessons are reserved for exam preparation or students with special educational needs. We also offer fully asynchronous learning for students without reliable internet for live classes.

We do not set fixed lesson dates. Instead, teachers and students are matched according to their availability.

Sessions usually take place in the morning, in the afternoon, or at weekends. We generally advise against evening or night-time sessions because these can be difficult for students in Gaza. However, the final schedule depends on everyone’s circumstances, and we match volunteers and students with similar availability.

Live lessons are typically conducted through Google Meet, Zoom, or Microsoft Teams. WhatsApp is also used for ongoing communication because it is the most widely used communication tool in Palestine.

Yes. Teachers are expected to plan their lessons using the textbook and curated resources we provide, as well as any suitable resources of their own. Conversation partners should also prepare topics and prompts so that their conversations have a clear purpose.

Time-zone changes do not alter the programme’s general expectations. For Ramadan and public holidays, agree any changes directly with your students. We do not impose a single schedule because arrangements depend on each group’s circumstances.

04

Time & Commitment

Weekly volunteering and what happens after the initial term.

Certainly. If you are available and can commit for three months, you may choose to volunteer for more than 90 minutes each week.

Three months is the minimum period for which we ask teachers to volunteer. However, we would be delighted if you chose to continue teaching your student beyond the initial term.

Volunteers are expected to complete the minimum commitment set out in the code of conduct. This consistency is important for students’ stability. If exceptional circumstances make this impossible, contact us as soon as possible.

Certificates and awards are provided when volunteers reach the relevant milestones. A certificate depends on completing the required number of weeks and, for teachers, fulfilling the student-assessment requirements. References are considered individually rather than issued automatically.

05

After Matching & Ongoing Support

Communication, scheduling difficulties, attendance, and support with your student.

First, read the teacher handbook carefully, as it contains the information you need. Then contact your student or group and arrange an introductory meeting.

Begin by checking the teacher handbook, textbook, Google Classroom, and online resources. If you still cannot find the information you need, contact the Ramzunā Languages team for support.

You may communicate with students through WhatsApp or another mutually agreed platform; WhatsApp is usually the easiest option. Ramzunā Languages communicates with volunteers by email, so you must check your inbox regularly.

Wait four days after your message. If you still have not received a response, contact us so that we can follow up.

Continue discussing possible times and look for a workable compromise. If you genuinely cannot find a suitable arrangement, email us for support.

This can be normal at the beginning. Trust and confidence take time to develop. Be patient, avoid putting the student under pressure, and use topics and activities connected to their interests. If the situation does not improve, contact us for advice.

Yes, once you have completed the minimum three-month commitment with your current student. If a serious difficulty arises before then, contact us to discuss the situation.

Yes. If you have enough availability to make an additional commitment, contact us and we will arrange another match where possible.

No. We cannot guarantee matches based on personal criteria such as age or marital status. Matches are made primarily according to availability and programme needs.

Let us know and continue teaching the other students in the group. We will try to add another student, particularly when the withdrawal happens near the beginning of the term.

Yes. Contact us and we will discuss adding suitable students to create a group.

Adapt your lesson content, instructions, and activities to the level you observe. Use the assessment resource where applicable, and focus on the student’s demonstrated needs rather than the original level label.

06

Mini-Groups & Teaching Practice

Group size, communication, participation, mixed levels, feedback, and teaching resources.

Mini-groups typically contain three to four students.

We recommend creating a WhatsApp group chat for the volunteer and all mini-group members. This keeps scheduling, reminders, materials, and asynchronous activities in one shared place.

Never force a student to speak. Use a trauma-informed approach: acknowledge them, give them time, offer low-pressure ways to contribute, and invite rather than demand participation. For example, let them answer in the chat, choose between two options, or prepare before speaking.

Use interactive activities such as debates, role plays, polls, games, collaborative tasks, and student-led questions. Ask open-ended questions, connect topics to students’ interests, and make sure each student has meaningful opportunities to contribute.

Vary the pace and activity type, give each student a clear role, and avoid allowing one person to dominate. Use pair exchanges, turn-taking, shared problem-solving, and brief individual tasks. Build activities around the students’ interests and celebrate progress.

Keep the same overall topic but adjust the level of support and challenge. Give students differentiated tasks, ask questions of varying difficulty, provide sentence starters where needed, and offer extension activities to more advanced students.

You will receive access to Google Classroom and a curated list of teaching resources, activities, and useful websites.

No. We provide a textbook and curated resources, but volunteers are responsible for creating session plans that suit their group.

Give regular positive feedback and recognise each student’s progress. Do not correct every mistake, as this can discourage participation. Focus on errors that affect understanding, recur frequently, or relate directly to the lesson’s language focus. At the end of the session, briefly highlight what went well and one or two priorities for improvement.

07

Safeguarding & Privacy

Reporting concerns and protecting students’ personal information.

Report the concern as soon as possible by emailing safeguarding@ramzunalanguages.org. Do not investigate the situation yourself or promise complete secrecy; record what was shared accurately and follow the safeguarding guidance in the teacher handbook.

All personal information about students must remain confidential, especially their names, phone numbers, contact details, circumstances, and anything they share privately. Do not publish or forward this information outside the authorised programme channels.

08

Attendance & Session Disruptions

Cancellations, non-attendance, technical problems, and continuity through asynchronous learning.

Attendance forms help us confirm that sessions are taking place and identify where support may be needed. They also provide programme data that allows us to evaluate performance, report our impact, and support grant applications.

Give your students at least 24 hours’ notice whenever possible. Agree on a new time and arrange a make-up session for every cancelled lesson.

Speak with the student first to understand what is happening and try to resolve the situation together. If the problem continues, email us. We will follow up and, where necessary, arrange another match.

You do not need to wait longer than 15 minutes. If it was a lesson, send the planned material asynchronously. If it was a conversation session, post two or three planned questions in the group chat and tell students when you will review or respond to their answers, so the missed session does not disrupt everyone’s schedule.

Record the absence in the attendance form and continue with the available students. Reschedule only if everyone in the group prefers to do so and can agree on another suitable time.

When technical difficulties prevent a live lesson, continue learning asynchronously. Send structured lesson activities consistently, or maintain a planned asynchronous conversation through an agreed messaging platform. Keep the work purposeful and connected to the student’s learning objectives.

Learning a new language is not only about the hour you spend with your tutor each week — it is about what you do with the other 167 hours. Students who make the fastest progress in our programme share a few simple habits that anyone can build into a busy week.

## Practise a Little, Every Day

Fifteen minutes of daily practice beats two hours once a week. Short, consistent sessions help your brain retain vocabulary and grammar far better than occasional cramming. Try reviewing five new words each morning and using them in a sentence before bed.

## Speak Out Loud, Even Alone

Reading silently is useful, but speaking out loud trains your mouth and ears together. Narrate your day in English as you go about it — describing what you are cooking, or what you see on your walk — builds fluency without needing a conversation partner every time.

## Use What You Already Love

If you enjoy football, follow English-language football commentary. If you love music, learn the lyrics to a few songs. Connecting new vocabulary to things you already care about makes it stick, and makes practice feel less like homework.

## Do Not Fear Mistakes

Every student in our programme, no matter how advanced, still makes mistakes in lessons. Volunteer educators expect this and are there to help, not to judge. The students who improve fastest are usually the ones who speak the most, mistakes included.

## Show Up to Every Lesson

Consistency with your volunteer educator matters more than any app or textbook. Missing lessons breaks momentum and makes it harder to build on what you learned the week before. If something comes up, message your educator to reschedule rather than skip entirely.

Small, steady habits compound over months into real fluency. Your volunteer educator is there to guide you — the rest is showing up, a little each day.

<p style="text-align: justify;">
After crafting your topic sentence, build your essay with strong supporting details. These key pieces of evidence are crucial for making your essay persuasive or informative.  A typical body paragraph consists of a topic sentence, supporting evidence, analysis and a linking sentence.
</p>

## The three types of supporting detail

<p style="text-align: justify;">
You can support a topic sentence in three main ways.
</p>

<ol style="text-align: justify;">
  <li><strong>Facts.</strong> Any evidence you have that is true.</li>
  <li><strong>Logic.</strong> Reasoning and common sense that persuade and convince your reader.</li>
  <li><strong>Expert opinions.</strong> The views of people who are experts in a relevant field.</li>
</ol>

## Why each one works

<ul style="text-align: justify;">
  <li><strong>The fact</strong> The fact is about language learners, showing that the ones making the most progress are the those who practice for just 15 minutes every single day .</li>
  <li><strong>The logic</strong> is not something you can look up, but the reasoning makes sense to your audience. Of course someone who practices every day can correct mistakes and makes progress faster, and that gives the reader a reason to believe the topic sentence.</li>
  <li><strong>The expert opinion</strong> works because Psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus is an  German psychologist, so we can assume he knows what he is talking about. He is a good authority on what it takes to retain information.</li>
</ul>

## Quick checklist

<ul style="text-align: justify;">
  <li>Does each detail directly support my topic sentence?</li>
  <li>Is my fact true?</li>
  <li>Would my audience agree that my logic makes sense?</li>
  <li>Is my expert actually an expert in a relevant field?</li>
</ul>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
Supporting details prove your point, but they do not explain it. The next step in the paragraph is analysis: telling the reader what your evidence means and why it matters.
</p>
